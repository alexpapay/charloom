How to convert images to ASCII art

Charloom is a free, open-source image to ASCII art converter. It turns photos and illustrations into letters, digits and symbols. Each character represents the light in part of an image. Optional opacity adds shadows while keeping a single ink color.

Try the converter with the orbital demo or upload your own PNG, JPEG or WebP. No account is required.

Make your first text picture

  1. Choose Open an image. Online uploads support files up to 8 MB and 16 megapixels.
  2. Select a character palette and width. Start at 120 columns for a portrait. Choose 40 or 72 for a small terminal, or 180–360 for finer detail. Custom widths support 2–400 columns; very tall images may need fewer columns.
  3. Adjust contrast, gamma and contour emphasis, then press Weave image.
  4. Adjust artwork ink, background color, bold and shadow strength live. Show original compares your source with the character output.
  5. Choose Export text for TXT, HTML with tones, JSON or clipboard copy. Export image saves a PNG with a transparent or selected background.

Which images work best?

Choose a clear silhouette, simple background and enough light to separate important features. Crop portraits close enough to see the eyes, glasses and mouth. A larger source cannot recover details that occupy less than one character cell. More columns preserve more detail but require smaller characters at the same display size.

Charloom accounts for the shape of character cells to preserve source proportions. Use a monospace font and appropriate line spacing when displaying exported text. Proportional fonts or different line heights can stretch the picture.

Contrast, gamma and shadows

Contrast separates light and dark regions. Gamma adjusts midtones and can reveal facial features hidden in shadow. Contour emphasis strengthens edges; too much may make textured backgrounds noisy. Change one setting at a time and compare with the source.

Shadow strength controls opacity independently of the characters. At 0%, visible characters share one opacity for classic ASCII. Higher values make dark cells more transparent. One ink color makes the artwork easy to recolor.

Which export should I use?

PNG exports a raster image of the characters at a selected size (1024, 2048 or 4096 pixels on the longest edge, or a custom longest edge), preserving proportions. Choose a transparent background to overlay the art, or use your selected background color. PNG generation stays in your browser.

  • TXT contains plain characters for terminals and text editors. It carries no color or opacity.
  • HTML + tones preserves color, opacity and cell proportions for web pages.
  • JSON includes tone values and sizes generated with the current settings for use in your own software.

Optional AI preparation

The converter itself does not use AI. AI redraw prompt offers an optional recipe for an external image editor: bold contours, broad grayscale tones and simplified texture. Attach your image in that editor, check the redraw preserves identity and proportions, then upload it here.

Can I work offline?

Yes. The same Python engine runs locally. Follow the CLI installation instructions for private or batch workflows. Online uploads go to the server, are processed in memory and are not saved by the converter. See privacy details.
